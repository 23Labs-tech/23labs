The 23Labs website was rebuilt so it describes the work we actually do. The earlier site had been through several designs, and parts of it no longer matched the studio. This note covers why we changed it, what is on it now, and who it is written for.

## Why it needed a rebuild

23Labs is a Melbourne studio. We build software, integrations and automation for small and medium businesses across Australia and New Zealand. The people who get in touch are usually business owners and operations managers. They have a job that still depends on someone remembering the next step, and they want to know whether that job can be taken off the team.

The first public version of this site went up in June 2026. After that it kept moving: a repositioning around automation and software, new service pages, then a stretch of visual redesigns. What lagged was the content. A visitor could not easily see which problem we take on, which service fits it, or what a finished project looks like. The rebuild closes that gap with pages, not a slogan.

## Who it is for

The pages are written for small and medium businesses in Australia and New Zealand, and for the owners and operations managers inside them.

The problems are consistent, whatever the industry:

- Manual processes, where the work only moves because someone chases it.
- Disconnected systems, where the same details are typed into a second tool.
- Repetitive admin: follow-ups, data entry and status updates that fill the week.
- Tools the business has outgrown, often a spreadsheet or a product that only covers part of the job.

## What changed

These are the pieces that are on the site now.

**Six service pages.** Each offer has its own page: [Business Process Automation](/services/business-process-automation), [AI Workflow Automation](/services/ai-workflow-automation), [Custom Software Development](/services/custom-software-development), [AI Agents](/services/ai-agents), [System Integrations](/services/system-integrations) and [Ongoing Support](/services/ongoing-support). System Integrations and Ongoing Support were added so those two were not folded into a broader software page. There is no single services index. The navigation lists the six pages directly.

**Six industry pages.** [Construction](/construction), [transport and logistics](/freight-logistics), [professional services](/professional-services), [allied health](/allied-health), [real estate](/real-estate) and [trades and field services](/trades-field-services) each have a standalone page, with an [industries](/industries) overview in front of them. The transport and logistics address stays `/freight-logistics`.

**Case studies.** The [work](/work) page collects the projects we can show. Each one is a full write-up: [CorIT Tech](/case-studies/corit-tech), where customer details in the CRM create the project and its tasks; [Huntress Recruitment](/case-studies/huntress-recruitment), where Bullhorn stays the system of record for SEEK and the website; [a real estate office](/case-studies/real-estate-workflow-automation), where an enquiry updates the property record; and [Lily Olsen Counselling](/case-studies/lily-olsen-counselling), a four-page website rebuild. The CorIT page is also the source of the one time figure on this site: a reported saving of more than 10 hours of onboarding admin a month.

**The practical parts around the pages.** This blog is where the longer explanations live. The [contact form](/contact) sends an enquiry through, with checks on the server, rather than only opening an email app. Pages carry titles, descriptions, a sitemap and structured data so they can be found and shared. The visual design is the current blue and white system, and the earlier illustrations on the service and industry pages were replaced with photography.

## How those problems map to the work

A page is only useful if it points at the right kind of help.

- A manual process with the same steps every time is [business process automation](/services/business-process-automation).
- Two systems that will not share a record are [system integrations](/services/system-integrations).
- Repetitive work that means reading documents, sorting enquiries or drafting the same reply is [AI workflow automation](/services/ai-workflow-automation).
- A tool the business has outgrown, where another off-the-shelf product would only add a workaround, is a [custom software](/services/custom-software-development) conversation.
- A first response for customers or staff, with a person still taking anything that needs judgement, is [AI agents](/services/ai-agents).
- Something already built that has to keep working as the business changes sits with [ongoing support](/services/ongoing-support).

None of these is a reason to replace everything you already pay for. Often the useful change is smaller: connect two tools, or take one repeated task off the team. The [work](/work) page is the place to see what that has looked like on real projects.
