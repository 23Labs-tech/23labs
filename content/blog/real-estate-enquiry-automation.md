A busy real estate office receives enquiries from everywhere: portals, the office website, phone calls, emails, open home sign-ins and social media. Some are buyers, some are potential vendors, some are tenants or landlords and some are asking for an appraisal. Each one needs to reach the right person and end up against the right property and contact record.

When that routing depends on someone reading every enquiry and forwarding it, things slip. Enquiries sit in a shared inbox, go to an agent who's away or never get linked to the property. This article focuses on routing and records, rather than reply speed, and how automation can make sure every enquiry lands in the right place.

## The short answer

Bring enquiries into a small number of entry points, classify each one by type and property, route it to the right person with clear rules and record it against the right contact and property. Escalate anything that isn't picked up. You usually don't need to replace your CRM to do this.

## Where enquiries go wrong

These are the common failure points:

- **The shared inbox.** Enquiries pile up in one place, and everyone assumes someone else has dealt with them.
- **Unclear ownership.** It isn't obvious whether an enquiry belongs to the listing agent, the property manager or the person on duty.
- **Missing property links.** An enquiry is answered, but never linked to the property record, so the agent can't see the full picture.
- **Duplicate contacts.** The same person enquires through two channels and ends up as two records.
- **Nobody follows up.** Enquiries that aren't picked up don't trigger any reminder or escalation.

## Classify first: what kind of enquiry is it?

Good routing starts with knowing what you're routing. Most enquiries fall into a few types:

- **Buyer enquiries** about a listed property.
- **Vendor and appraisal requests** from people thinking about selling.
- **Rental enquiries** from prospective tenants.
- **Landlord enquiries** about property management.
- **General enquiries** that don't fit elsewhere.

Some of this can be read from the source, such as which form or listing it came from. Where it can't, AI can help suggest a category, as long as uncertain cases go to a person.

## Routing rules that work

Once you know the type, routing rules decide where it goes. Common rules include:

- **Listing agent.** Buyer enquiries about a property go to the agent responsible for that listing.
- **Property management team.** Rental and landlord enquiries go to the right property manager or team.
- **Area or roster.** Appraisal requests go to the agent for that area, or to whoever is on duty.
- **Backup when someone's away.** If the assigned person is on leave, the enquiry goes to a named backup.
- **Escalation.** If an enquiry isn't acknowledged within a time you set, it's escalated to the sales or office manager.

Keep the rules simple enough that the team understands them. Complex rules are hard to trust and hard to maintain.

## Records: link every enquiry to a contact and property

Routing gets the enquiry to the right person. Records make sure it isn't lost. For each enquiry, the automation should:

- **Find or create the contact,** checking for an existing record first to avoid duplicates.
- **Link it to the property,** where it's about a specific listing or address.
- **Log the enquiry and its source,** so you can see where your enquiries are coming from.
- **Create a follow-up task,** assigned to the person responsible.

Before designing any of this, check what your CRM and portals already support for receiving and matching leads. Many CRMs have their own lead handling features, and the right approach depends on what you already have.

## Privacy basics for real estate offices

Enquiries contain personal information, so it's worth getting the basics right.

- **Collect what you need.** Only ask for the information required to deal with the enquiry.
- **Be clear about use.** Tell people how their information will be used, through your privacy policy and collection notices.
- **Control access.** Make sure only the right people can see enquiry details.
- **Know whether you're covered.** Many small businesses with an annual turnover of $3 million or less are exempt from the Privacy Act, but there are exceptions. According to the OAIC's [guidance for reporting entities under the AML/CTF Act](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/privacy-guidance-for-organisations-and-government-agencies/organisations/privacy-guidance-for-reporting-entities-under-the-anti-money-laundering-and-counter-terrorism-financing-act), real estate professionals are among the businesses that become reporting entities from 1 July 2026, and reporting entities are covered by the Privacy Act for their AML/CTF-related activities even if they're small businesses.

The OAIC's [overview of the Australian Privacy Principles](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles) is a good starting point. This is general information, not legal advice, so talk to your adviser about how the rules apply to your office.

## When automation isn't the answer

Automation won't help much if:

- **Ownership is unclear.** If the team hasn't agreed who handles which enquiries, automating the routing just moves the confusion.
- **Your CRM already does it.** If your CRM's lead handling covers your routing, configure that first.
- **Volume is low.** A small office with a handful of enquiries a week may do fine with a clear roster and a shared checklist.
- **The data is messy.** If contacts and properties are heavily duplicated, clean them up before automating.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do we need a new CRM?

Usually not. In our real estate work, the aim was to connect existing tools rather than replace them. Start by checking what your current CRM supports.

### Can portal enquiries be routed automatically?

It depends on how enquiries reach you and what your CRM supports. Check what your portals and CRM offer for lead delivery and matching before designing anything custom.

### How do we handle duplicate contacts?

Check for existing contacts by email and phone before creating a new one, and flag possible matches for someone to review rather than merging automatically.

### What happens when an agent is away?

Set a named backup for each person or team. The routing rules should send enquiries to the backup automatically during leave.

### Is AI needed for this?

Not always. Many enquiries can be classified from their source. AI can help with free-text enquiries, as long as uncertain ones go to a person.
