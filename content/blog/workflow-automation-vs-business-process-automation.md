## Workflow automation vs business process automation

Workflow automation and business process automation are closely related, but they are not exactly the same thing.

Workflow automation usually focuses on a defined sequence of tasks, such as routing an enquiry, approving a document or updating a record.

Business process automation looks at a broader end-to-end business process that may include several workflows, systems, teams, approvals and exceptions. Our introduction to [what business process automation is](/blog/what-is-business-process-automation) covers it in more depth.

The distinction matters because businesses sometimes try to solve a process-level problem with one small automation, or overbuild a full process solution when one workflow would be enough.

## The simple difference

**Scope**

- Workflow automation: one defined sequence of tasks
- Business process automation: an end-to-end business process

**Typical systems**

- Workflow automation: one or a few
- Business process automation: often several systems and teams

**Example**

- Workflow automation: route a new enquiry to the right salesperson
- Business process automation: manage the full lead-to-customer onboarding process

**Best for**

- Workflow automation: specific repetitive handoffs
- Business process automation: broader operational bottlenecks

Both approaches can be useful. The right one depends on where the actual problem sits.

## What is workflow automation?

A workflow is a sequence of steps that moves work from one state to another.

For example:

A website enquiry arrives → a CRM record is created → the lead is assigned → the salesperson is notified → a follow-up task is created.

That is a workflow.

Automating it means the predictable steps happen without someone manually copying information, assigning the record and setting reminders.

Other workflow automation examples include:

- routing invoices for approval
- notifying a team when a job status changes
- creating a project when a deal is won
- sending a document for signature
- moving information from one application to another
- generating a routine report on a schedule

The scope is usually clear and contained.

## What is business process automation?

A business process is larger than a single workflow.

Take customer onboarding.

The overall process may include:

- the sales handoff
- customer data collection
- contract completion
- CRM updates
- accounting setup
- project creation
- access provisioning
- internal notifications
- customer communications
- initial delivery tasks

Each of those areas may contain its own workflow.

Business process automation looks across the entire chain to reduce unnecessary manual work, improve handoffs and make sure information moves correctly between systems and teams. The [CorIT Tech case study](/case-studies/corit-tech) is a real example of improving the handoff from a won client into project delivery.

It is less about one trigger and action, and more about designing how the process should operate as a whole.

## A practical example: quoting

Imagine a business has a slow quoting process. This is an illustrative example.

### Workflow-level solution

The immediate problem may be that completed quotes wait too long for approval.

A workflow automation could route any quote above a certain value to the correct manager, notify them and remind them if it sits untouched.

That may be enough.

### Process-level solution

But the broader problem may involve much more:

- enquiries arrive in several formats
- customer information is re-entered manually
- estimators search multiple systems for data
- approvals happen over email
- quote documents are built manually
- the CRM is not updated consistently
- follow-ups depend on memory

In that case, fixing only the approval workflow will not solve the real problem.

A broader process automation project may need to improve the entire quote journey from intake through approval, sending, follow-up and handoff after acceptance. We go through that journey step by step in [how to automate a manual quoting process](/blog/automate-manual-quoting-process).

## Where systems integration fits

Automation often depends on systems being able to exchange information.

If your CRM, accounting software, operations platform and custom tools do not communicate, the process will still contain manual handoffs even if individual tasks are automated.

[Systems integration](/services/system-integrations) is the layer that allows information to move between those applications.

That may happen through:

- native integrations
- APIs
- webhooks
- automation platforms
- custom middleware
- scheduled data synchronisation

In many projects, integration and automation are not separate problems. They are two parts of the same solution. If two of your tools refuse to talk to each other, see [what to do when your business software doesn't integrate](/blog/business-software-does-not-integrate).

## Where AI workflow automation fits

Traditional workflow automation works best when the input is structured and the rules are clear.

For example: if a deal reaches a certain stage, create a project.

AI becomes useful when the workflow includes information that is harder to handle with fixed rules.

Examples include:

- reading an incoming email and classifying its intent
- extracting fields from a document
- summarising a request before routing it
- identifying information from inconsistent text
- preparing a draft response for staff review

The AI component can interpret the information, while standard automation handles what happens next.

AI does not replace the workflow. It becomes one step inside it. That is the approach behind our [AI workflow automation](/services/ai-workflow-automation) work.

## Which level of automation do you actually need?

Start with the problem, not the terminology.

### Choose a focused workflow automation when:

- one specific handoff is causing friction
- the workflow is easy to define
- only a small number of systems are involved
- fixing that workflow would create a meaningful improvement
- the surrounding process already works reasonably well

### Look at broader business process automation when:

- the same information is repeatedly re-entered
- several departments are involved
- multiple disconnected systems contribute to delays
- work gets lost between stages
- nobody has a clear view of the end-to-end process
- improving one step would simply move the bottleneck somewhere else

## Do not automate more than you need

It is easy to turn a contained problem into a large technology project.

If one workflow is broken, fix the workflow first.

A broader process redesign makes sense when the surrounding process genuinely needs it, not because "business process automation" sounds more comprehensive.

The same rule applies in the other direction. If the problem is spread across the whole process, adding one small automation may create a temporary improvement without addressing the underlying issue.

## When this is not the answer

Neither workflow automation nor business process automation will solve problems caused by:

- unclear responsibilities
- poor underlying data
- unnecessary process steps
- software that is fundamentally unfit for the job
- a process that changes every week
- work that requires human judgement almost every time

Sometimes the right answer is to simplify the process, train the team, change the software or leave a step manual. If you suspect the software itself is the issue, read [should you automate a process or replace the software?](/blog/automate-process-or-replace-software)

## Frequently asked questions

### Is workflow automation part of business process automation?

Often, yes. A broader business process may contain several individual automated workflows.

### Which is cheaper: workflow automation or business process automation?

A focused workflow is usually smaller in scope, but cost depends on the systems, complexity and exception handling involved. A single workflow involving a difficult legacy system can still require significant work. Our article on [what business automation costs in Australia](/blog/how-much-does-business-automation-cost) explains the main cost drivers.

### Do I need a business process management platform?

Not automatically. Many businesses can improve processes by connecting the tools they already use. A dedicated platform is useful only when the business requirements justify it.

### Can AI replace workflow automation?

No. AI can interpret information within a workflow, but the surrounding rules, integrations, approvals and actions still need to be designed.
